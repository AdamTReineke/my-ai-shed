# 8 — Fastener Schedule

All fasteners in contact with PT lumber or exterior exposure must be **stainless steel** or **hot-dip galvanized (HDG)**. ACQ-treated wood corrodes plain steel and electroplated (zinc-plated) fasteners. Stainless is preferred for longevity (50-year design life); HDG is acceptable for interior/protected connections.

**Nail gun note:** Most connections below call for hand-nailing or a framing nailer. For 16d HDG nails, a full-head framing nailer (e.g., Bostitch F21PL) is ideal. Ring-shank nails for sheathing can use a coil nailer. Verify your nailer accepts HDG/SS nail strips before buying.

## 1. Pier to Beam/Post — 6x ABU66SS

Simpson ABU66SS stainless adjustable post base, one per pier (6 total).

- 1/2" × 7" SS wedge anchor
  - Qty: 1 per bracket × 6 piers = 6 total
  - In contact with: cured concrete, SS bracket
  - Material depth: min 3.5" embedment in concrete (ABU66SS spec); 7" provides ample embedment with head above bracket
  - Drill 7/16" hole, set anchor, torque to 40 ft-lbs

- 1/2" × 4.5" SS hex bolt + nut + 2 washers
  - Qty: 2 per bracket × 6 piers = 12 sets
  - In contact with: PT Hem-Fir beam/post, SS bracket
  - Material depth: beam/post face is 5.5" (6×6 actual); bolt passes through bracket flanges (~5/16" each side) and one face of the beam — not a through-bolt, flanges bolt to the side face. 4.5" is sufficient for the side-flange connection per ABU66SS template.
  - Through ABU side flanges into beam/post; SS required for PT contact

## 3. Beam Lamination — 2 beams × 16'

3-ply built-up beam: 2×10 + 3×10 + 2×10 (5.5" × 9.25"), per [2-beam.md](2-beam.md).

### Recommended: Structural Screws

- GRK RSS 5/16" × 3-1/8" structural screw (HDG-compatible coating)
  - Qty: ~30 per beam × 2 beams = ~60 total
  - In contact with: PT Hem-Fir 2×10 outer plies, PT Hem-Fir 3×10 center ply
  - Material depth: outer ply 1.5" + center ply 2.5" = 4.0" total; 3-1/8" screw penetrates 1.625" into center ply, which is adequate (GRK RSS spec requires ≥1.5" penetration into main member)
  - Drive from both faces, staggered 12" o.c.; GRK RSS screws are approved for PT lumber with ACQ treatment.

### Alternative A: Through-Bolts

- 1/2" × 5.5" HDG carriage bolt + nut + 2 washers
  - Qty: ~8 per beam × 2 beams = ~16 sets
  - In contact with: PT Hem-Fir 2×10 and 3×10 plies through full 5.5" width
  - Material depth: total beam width = 1.5" + 2.5" + 1.5" = 5.5"; bolt length matches exactly — add 1/4" thread engagement minimum, so 5.5" is correct with nut flush or just proud
  - Stagger top/bottom at 24" o.c.; clamp all three plies in one pass. Stronger mechanical clamping and fully reversible, but requires drilling through 5.5" with a long-shank bit and adds ~$20/beam. Good choice if you want the most bomber connection.

### Alternative B: Nails + Adhesive (budget)

- 10d HDG ring-shank nail (3" × 0.148")
  - Qty: ~30 per face per beam × 2 faces × 2 beams = ~120 total
  - In contact with: PT Hem-Fir plies
  - Material depth: outer ply 1.5" + center ply 2.5" = 4.0"; 3" nail penetrates 1.5" into center ply, clinches if it exits the far side
  - From both faces at 12" o.c. staggered. Add construction adhesive between plies. Lower cost but harder to drive consistently and less clamping force than screws.

## 4. Joist to Beam — 26x H2.5ASS Hurricane Ties

Simpson H2.5ASS (18-ga stainless), one per joist-beam intersection. Joists sit on top of beams (joist-over-beam framing). 13 joists × 2 beams = 26 ties.

- 0.131" × 2-1/2" SS joist hanger nail
  - Qty: 10 per tie × 26 ties = 260 total
  - In contact with: PT Hem-Fir joist face, PT Hem-Fir beam top, SS tie
  - Material depth: joist face is 1.5" (2×10 width); beam top is 3.5" (3-ply built-up); 2-1/2" nail gives 2.0" penetration into either member — correct Simpson-specified nail for this tie
  - 5 into joist face + 5 into beam top; SS required — tie is stainless and lumber is PT

Allowable loads (SPF/HF, 160% duration): Uplift 380 lbs, Lateral F₁ 75 lbs, Lateral F₂ 70 lbs.

*Do NOT substitute screws for nails in hurricane ties unless the screw is Simpson-approved (e.g., SD connector screws). Standard drywall or deck screws are brittle and will snap under shear.*

## 11. Truss Assembly — Plywood Gussets

9 queen-post trusses, per [6-trusses.md](6-trusses.md). Built with 1/2" structural plywood gussets on both sides of each joint, glued and nailed.

### Recommended: Plywood Gussets + Adhesive

- 8d bright common nail (2.5") or #8 × 2" bright steel screw
  - Qty: ~20 fasteners per gusset × ~126 gussets (9 trusses × 8 joints × 2 sides) = ~2,500 fasteners
  - In contact with: KD SPF/Hem-Fir truss members (interior, non-PT), 1/2" structural plywood gusset
  - Material depth: 1/2" gusset + 1.5" truss member = 2.0" minimum; 2.5" nail gives 2.0" penetration into member; 2" screw gives 1.5" penetration into member (minimum acceptable). Do not use 1-5/8" screws — only 1-1/8" penetration into the member.
  - Bright (plain steel) is fine — trusses are interior/protected. 3" o.c. around gusset perimeter, 1/2" edge distance.
  - Plus: construction adhesive (PL Premium or Titebond III) full coverage under each gusset. Critical — adhesive carries most of the shear.

Gusset sizes per joint:
- Peak: 12" × 12" — 1 per truss, 18 total (9 trusses × 2 sides)
- Queen post tops: 10" × 8" — 2 per truss, 36 total
- Queen post bottoms: 8" × 8" — 2 per truss, 36 total
- Eave joints: 8" × 10" — 2 per truss, 36 total

### Alternative: Metal Connector Plates

- Simpson TP37 truss plate (or equivalent gang-nail plate) at each joint
  - Qty: 2 per joint × 8 joints × 9 trusses = 144 plates
  - In contact with: KD truss members (non-PT)
  - Driven with a plate press or heavy dead-blow mallet. No adhesive or separate fasteners needed; cleaner appearance. Downsides: plates are expensive (~$3–5 each = ~$500+), require accurate tooth engagement, and are harder to position than gussets without a dedicated jig. Not recommended for a one-time DIY build — plywood gussets are more forgiving and use material already on-site.

## Notes

- PT compatibility: All fasteners touching pressure-treated wood must be SS or HDG. ACQ treatment corrodes plain steel and electro-galvanized (EG) coatings. This applies to: pier hardware, beam screws/bolts, joist ties, rim joist nails, bottom plate nails, subfloor nails, blocking nails, and furring screws.
- Interior framing exception: Wall studs, top plates, headers, and trusses are standard (non-PT) KD lumber protected from weather. Bright (plain steel) nails are acceptable for these connections.
- Simpson fastener rules: Always use the fastener type and size specified by Simpson for their connectors. Never substitute screws for nails in hurricane ties or joist hangers unless using Simpson SD connector screws.
- Nail sizes: 16d common = 3.5", 10d common = 3", 8d common = 2.5". "Common" has a thicker shank than "box" or "sinker" — use common for structural connections.
- Zip System note: The 24 sheets of Zip System panels from receipts.csv replace the plain OSB originally specified in §10. Zip panels are nailed the same way; the integrated water-resistive barrier means housewrap behind the siding is optional (though Tyvek was also purchased — use it at the sill and jamb flashings at minimum).
